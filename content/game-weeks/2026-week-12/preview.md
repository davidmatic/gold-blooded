The 49ers come home at 9-1 after a 35-10 win over Minnesota in Mexico City, and the reward is a rematch with a Seattle team they edged 32-29 on the road back in Week 5. This one won't be easy. The Seahawks come in off a bye, which Kyle Shanahan noted gives them a seven-day rest advantage after the 49ers spent two straight weeks away from home. Shanahan called Seattle a heavy two-shell defense and did not hold back on how good it is: "They never give you an easy one. Every coverage is tight."

The headline on Wednesday's injury report is Brock Purdy, who was a DNP with an ankle injury suffered in the second quarter against the Vikings. That came days after he was named NFC Offensive Player of the Week for completing 21-of-26 passes for 255 yards and three touchdowns. Shanahan said it is not a high ankle sprain and called Purdy day-to-day, but admitted there was concern since he couldn't go. Thursday and Friday practice will tell us a lot.

Not every DNP is a worry. Christian McCaffrey and Trent Williams sat Wednesday as resting players, and Keenan Allen was limited for the same reason. Mike Evans (Achilles) was a DNP too, and Shanahan said Evans would probably never practice on Wednesdays regardless. Dre Greenlaw (neck) was limited, and Shanahan said he feels better about him this week than last. Deommodore Lenoir (foot) and Upton Stout (shoulder, neck) were both limited, while Jordan Watkins (groin) and Siran Neal (knee, foot) did not practice.

The offensive line has a new look. Colton McKivitz, who started 10 games this season, went to injured reserve, and Shanahan confirmed Vederian Lowe will be the starting right tackle going forward. Austen Pleasants, limited with a knee, is a possibility to be activated, and Nate Hobbs is back off IR to add depth at corner.

On the other side, Raheem Morris has to account for a Seattle offense he says has played well since the first meeting, whether Sam Darnold or Drew Lock is at quarterback, plus rookie-style speed from running back Jadarian Price, who did not play in Week 5. Add the chance of rain at Levi's Stadium and this has the feel of a tight, physical divisional game.
